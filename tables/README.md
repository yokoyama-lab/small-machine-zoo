# Tables of the new URTMs

Plain-text quintuple tables of the seven new universal reversible Turing machines
of the paper, and of machines obtained later: URTM(4,40), which dominates URTM(4,41),
URTM(4,38), URTM(5,36), and URTM(6,29), which dominate URTM(4,40), URTM(5,37), and
URTM(6,30), URTM(9,9) and URTM(10,7), each with fewer states or fewer symbols than
Morita's URTM(10,8) (URTM(10,7) dominates it), and URTM(9,8), URTM(7,26), and
URTM(6,28), which dominate URTM(9,9), URTM(7,28), and URTM(6,29).
This file is the only description of them; each table file has a single comment line
that names the machine.

## Format

After the comment line (`%`), a file gives the initial state, the final states
(none), the states, the symbols, the blank, and one quintuple `[p, s, t, d, q]` per
line: in state `p` reading `s`, write `t`, move `d` (`-` left, `+` right), and enter
`q`. There are no halting quintuples; a machine halts where no quintuple applies.

## URTM(3,600)

Obtained from Morita's URTM(32,3) by the three-state conversion of the paper (one
symbol per cell, shared and pruned counters, mirrored). The states are `l`, `r0`,
`r1`; the initial state is `l`; the blank is `b`.

## The five machines obtained by symbol merging

URTM(4,41), URTM(5,37), URTM(6,30), URTM(7,28), and URTM(8,21) are obtained from
Morita's URTM(13,7) by block decomposition with `(b+,b-)` blocks, merging of roles,
and symbol merging (Section 3.3 of the paper). They are given without their two
halting quintuples; the formalization proves universality for this form as well
(Section 4 of the paper).

- Symbols: `b`, `y`, `n`, `y1`, `n1`, `*`, `$` are the symbols of URTM(13,7) (`y1`
  and `n1` are written Y and N in the paper), and `[i,s]` is the counter symbol with
  value `i` on `s`. Each merged symbol is named by the first symbol of its class.
- Roles: `H`, `O`, and `G` name the roles of a block: `H` is the stable role, `O` the
  origin side of a carry, and `G` its destination side; `+k` and `-k` are the k-th
  blocks of Q+ and Q- (Section 3.2 of the paper).
- Initial configuration: that of URTM(13,7), with the symbol `s` under the head
  replaced by the counter `[i,s]` given below and every symbol replaced by the first
  symbol of its class.
- In the merges of URTM(4,41) and URTM(5,37), three pairs of quintuples become the
  same quintuple; each of them is listed once, as in the formalization.

## URTM(4,40)

Obtained like the five machines above (block decomposition with `(b+,b-)` blocks,
merging of roles, and symbol merging), but from URTM(11,7) instead of URTM(13,7).
It is dominated by URTM(4,38) (below); the table is kept.
URTM(11,7) is obtained from Morita's URTM(13,7) by merging two states with tags on
cells and then merging symbols, twice (Section 3.4 of the later revision): copy-n-2
is merged into copy-end, and copy-y-2 into case-y-2 (the state names of Morita's
description file, with hyphens for underscores). No tags remain: its 59 quintuples
are those of URTM(13,7) with these two states renamed and with the read or the
written symbol changed to `n1` in 8 of them. Its symbols are those of URTM(13,7),
and it uses the encoding and the decoding of URTM(13,7). Its table is not
published here.

- Symbols and roles: named as for the five machines above; in `[i,s]`, `s` is a
  symbol of URTM(11,7).
- Blocks `(b+,b-) = (2,2)`; 143 quintuples, given without the two halting
  quintuples; the formalization proves universality for this form as well.
- The merge uses a relaxed condition: two quintuples of the same state may read
  symbols of the same class, and two quintuples entering the same state may write
  symbols of the same class, when the two become the same quintuple. Six pairs of
  quintuples become the same quintuple; each of them is listed once, as in the
  formalization.
- Merged classes: b = [7,$]; n = [7,y1]; y1 = [1,n]; n1 = [1,*]; * = [7,n1]; $ = [1,b]; [1,$] = [7,b].
- States and the roles they merge: state 0 = {H+1,G+1,G+2,O-1,O-2}; state 1 = {O+1,G-1,H-2}; state 2 = {H+2}; state 3 = {O+2,H-1,G-2}.
- Initial configuration: that of URTM(13,7), which URTM(11,7) also uses, with the
  symbol `s` under the head replaced by the counter `[7,s]` and every symbol
  replaced by the first symbol of its class; initial state 2.
- The two halting quintuples of URTM(11,7) correspond to: state 2 reading [1,$]; state 1 reading [3,b].

## URTM(4,38), URTM(5,36), and URTM(6,29)

Obtained like URTM(4,40) (block decomposition with `(b+,b-)` blocks, merging of
roles, and symbol merging under the relaxed condition), but from URTM(10,7) instead
of URTM(11,7). URTM(10,7) (below) is obtained from URTM(11,7) in the same way as URTM(11,7)
from URTM(13,7): the state cycle-end is merged with tags on cells into the state
that already contains case-y-2 and copy-y-2, and the symbols are then merged into
seven. Its symbols are `b`, `y`, `n`, `y1`, `*`, `$`, and `*_t3` (`*_t3` takes the
place of `n1`, which no longer occurs). It uses the encoding of URTM(13,7); its
decoding counts the separators written as either `*` or `y1`, and after the halting
quintuple that corresponds to `halt` of URTM(13,7) one `y1` more is present, which
the decoding does not count. Its table is `urtm10-7.txt` (below). The three tables
below are given without their two halting quintuples; the formalization proves
universality for this form as well.

- Symbols and roles: named as for the five machines above; in `[i,s]`, `s` is a
  symbol of URTM(10,7).
- Initial configuration: that of URTM(13,7), which URTM(10,7) also uses, with the
  symbol `s` under the head replaced by the counter `[i,s]` given below and every
  symbol replaced by the first symbol of its class.

## URTM(10,7)

Obtained from URTM(11,7) (see URTM(4,40) above) by merging the state cycle-end with
tags on cells into the state that already contains case-y-2 and copy-y-2, and then
merging the symbols into seven. No tags remain. It dominates Morita's URTM(10,8).

- States: the names of Morita's description file of URTM(13,7); `m_A_B` is the state
  obtained by merging `A` and `B`. The initial state is `begin_`.
- Symbols: `b`, `y`, `n`, `y1`, `*`, `$` of URTM(13,7), and `*_t3`, which takes the
  place of `n1` (no longer occurring).
- 55 quintuples, given without the two halting quintuples (state `begin_` reading `b`
  for `null`, state `case_y_1_` reading `b` for `halt`); the formalization proves
  universality for this form as well.
- Encoding: that of URTM(13,7). Decoding: that of URTM(13,7), except that the
  separators are counted when written as either `*` or `y1`, and after `halt` one
  `y1` more is present, which is not counted.

## URTM(9,9)

Obtained from Morita's URTM(10,8) by merging the state copy-y-2 with tags on cells
into case-y-1 (giving URTM(9,14), whose symbols are those of URTM(10,8) and their
tagged copies, written with a prime), and then merging symbols (it is dominated by
URTM(9,8) below; the table is kept): `b'` with `nd`, `y1'`
with `y1`, `n1'` with `n1`, `*'` with `y'`, and `$'` with `$`. It has one state fewer
and one symbol more than Morita's URTM(10,8).

- States: the names of Morita's description file of URTM(10,8); the initial state is
  `begin_`.
- Symbols: `b`, `y`, `n`, `nd`, `y1`, `n1`, `*`, `$` of URTM(10,8), and `y'`.
- 58 quintuples, given without the two halting quintuples (state `begin_` reading `b`
  for `null`, state `case_y_1_` reading `b` for `halt`); the formalization proves
  universality for this form as well.
- Encoding: that of URTM(10,8). Decoding: the merged symbols are read back according
  to the halting state (`nd` as `b`; in `null`, `y'` as `*`; in `halt`, `y'` as `y`),
  and the result is decoded as for URTM(10,8).

## URTM(9,8)

Obtained from URTM(10,7) (above) in the same way as URTM(10,7) from URTM(11,7): the
state copy-n-3 is merged with tags on cells into case-y-1 (the merged state is named
`m_case_y_1__copy_n_3_`), and the symbols are merged into eight at the same time. It
dominates URTM(9,9).

- States: as for URTM(10,7); the initial state is `begin_`.
- Symbols: `b`, `y`, `n`, `y1`, `*`, `$` of URTM(13,7), and two further symbols
  `*_t2` and `*_t3_t3`, each named after one member of its class in the merge (the
  names carry no meaning beyond that).
- 52 quintuples, given without the two halting quintuples (state `begin_` reading `b`
  for `null`, state `m_case_y_1__copy_n_3_` reading `b` for `halt`); the
  formalization proves universality for this form as well.
- Encoding and decoding: those of URTM(10,7).

## URTM(85,2)

Obtained from Morita's URTM(13,7) by the two-symbol conversion of the paper (3-bit
codes, path types, merging of equivalent states). The initial state is 2 and the
blank is 0.

- Encoding: a cell of URTM(13,7) is three consecutive cells; the symbols b, y, n, Y,
  N, *, $ of URTM(13,7) are written, from the left cell, as 000, 100, 101, 001, 010,
  110, 111. The head is on the leftmost of the three cells for the head cell of
  URTM(13,7).
- One step of URTM(13,7) is simulated in 3, 5, or 7 steps.
- The two halting quintuples of URTM(13,7) (reading the blank) correspond to state 2
  and state 7 reading 0.
